0. Context — why this report exists

This is a detailed, ambitious critique of the demo-client-dashboard (the "Zigbert" product) across the entire journey — sign-in → home → Pay report → Benefits report — from several perspectives but above all the client's. The goal is to understand what the product is, who it's for, where it falls short of "premium," and how to make it a product people genuinely love.

The brief gave full creative freedom, so this report is deliberately expansive and opinionated. It is a menu, not a contract — we'll pick what's worth building when we make the working copy. It is based on a full read of the codebase (auth/home/shell, the Pay SPA, the Benefits static app, and the design system).

0.1  Approved design direction (locked with the client)

Confirmed before finalising this report:

  • Branding — keep the existing identity, elevate the premium feel. Retain the current Zigbert palette: warm-cream canvas #F7F5F0, white cards, deep navy #0C1829, and gold #C9A84C as the signature accent. We are not changing the colours — the goal is to make the existing identity feel materially more premium (depth, finish, spacing, type discipline, richer micro-interactions, on-brand charts) and to remove the unfinished/template tells below.
  • Charts — on-brand. Navy + gold primary, slate muted, semantic red/amber for risk. Remove the off-brand Recharts indigo #6366f1.
  • Demo client identity — "Brighton Technologies". Replace both the generic clientName: "Demo" and the leftover "Penge Churches" in the Benefits report with one coherent demo persona, matching the Brighton-based roles already in the Pay data (a content fix, not a colour change).

1. What the product is (and what it's for)

Zigbert is a client-facing reward-intelligence portal built by TwentySix Consulting. A reward/HR client signs in with a username + password and lands on a calm "What do you want to do today?" home page with two doors:

  • Pay → a benchmarking dashboard: where each role sits against the market (lower quartile / median / upper quartile), role-by-role detail, market context (inflation, unemployment, pay-rise forecasts), bonus norms, and recommended next steps.
  • Benefits → a benchmarking report: how the client's benefits offer compares to the market across categories (core, time off, health & wellbeing, financial, ESG/DEI, L&D), with strengths and areas to watch.

Consultants (logged-in admins) can edit any text inline via an "Edit as Admin" CMS mode. The template is cloned per client by editing client/src/config/clientConfig.ts.

The real-world job it does: it turns a consulting engagement's findings into a living, branded, self-serve deliverable. Historically a reward consultancy hands over a PDF or a deck. Zigbert's bet is that an interactive portal is more premium, more sticky, and more upsell-friendly ("upgrade to bespoke analysis"). That's the product's reason to exist — and it's the lens the whole report is judged through.

Who the client actually is (personas)

  • The HR/Reward lead (primary user). Mid-senior, time-poor, not a data analyst. Wants: "are we paying fairly, where are my risks, and what do I do Monday morning?" — fast, defensible, board-ready.
  • The CFO / CEO (over-the-shoulder audience). ~90 seconds of attention. Wants the headline: are we over- or under-paying, what's it costing us, what's the risk?
  • The TwentySix consultant (admin/editor). Needs to stand up a client instance quickly, tailor the narrative, look impeccable, and create a natural path to more paid work.

The jobs-to-be-done

  • "Tell me where I stand" — positioning, at a glance.
  • "Tell me what's wrong / at risk" — the 2–3 things that matter.
  • "Tell me what to do and what it costs" — action + budget.
  • "Let me prove it to my board" — export, share, defend the methodology.
  • "Make me feel I'm in expensive, expert hands" — premium feel, trust.

The central insight

The product today is built around browsing data. A premium reward product should be built around delivering decisions. Most high-value recommendations push the experience from "here is your data, explore it" toward "here is what it means and what to do."

2. Overall verdict

The bones are genuinely good. The visual language, editorial typography, the quartile-band visualisation, the home-page hot bar and carousel, and the micro-interactions already read as a premium product on first glance. The login page in particular is a strong, confident first impression.

But it's a beautifully dressed template, not yet a product clients love. The gap is in four areas:

  • Coherence — effectively two different apps stitched together (React shell + Pay SPA in Inter; Benefits app in Plus Jakarta Sans/Space Grotesk), with full-page reloads between them and a leftover "Penge Churches" client name still hardcoded in the Benefits report.
  • Meaning over data — the dashboards show numbers but rarely tell the client what it means or what to do. The "insights" are static demo copy, not derived from the data.
  • Trust & finish — placeholder data everywhere, no methodology/sample-size signals, off-brand chart colours, no real PDF/board export, and unfinished flows (forgot-password, empty/error states).
  • Interactivity — tables and charts are read-only; no filtering, search, drill-down, scenario modelling, or comparison-over-time — the things that make a portal worth more than a PDF.

3. The experience, surface by surface

3.1  Sign-in LoginPage.tsx

Strong. Dual-panel editorial layout, warm palette, refined type, tagline "The market, decoded — role by role, benefit by benefit." The best-finished screen in the app.

Gaps that undercut "premium": hardcoded "demo" placeholder in the username field; no "forgot password" (only a generic support email); no show/hide password, no field-level validation, one generic error for every failure; a temp-auth fallback with hardcoded credentials in client code (must never ship to a real build); the left panel is static where it could feel alive.

3.2  Home / landing Home.tsx

Ambitious and mostly excellent. Time-of-day greeting, "live" status pill, big editorial headline, a 6-tile Hot Bar of KPIs, two large Pay/Benefits choice cards with auto-playing carousels, and a Highlights row.

Gaps: the Highlights and Hot Bar numbers are static demo copy, not data-derived (the single biggest "make it real" opportunity); the Benefits card just greys out with "Not in this package" where there should be an upsell; thin carousel captions; no clear "start here / your 3 actions" entry point.

3.3  Navigation & app shell App.tsx · Shell.tsx · Layout.tsx

The React app owns /, /account, /report/draft. /pay/* and /benefits/* are entirely separate static builds, reached by window.location.href — full page reloads, not SPA transitions. Two different nav chrome systems (top tabs for home; a 288px navy sidebar for Pay).

Gaps: the full-reload jump is the most jarring premium-breaking moment in the product; no breadcrumbs / "you are here" across the boundary; two nav models for one product; no global search / quick-jump.

3.4  Pay report pages/* → /pay/

Good coverage: Market Comparison, Role-by-Role detail cards, Risks/distribution, Market Context, Bonus, Benchmarking + a "Quartiles Explained" graphic, a Benefits-market breakdown, and a Next Steps page with the Targeted-vs-Bespoke upsell. The quartile band + "you are here" marker is intuitive and well reused; role cards are nicely composed; PNG/CSV export exist per chart.

Gaps: all data is fictional placeholder (12 roles, all "Brighton") with no "sample" framing; charts use off-brand indigo #6366f1; read-only with no drill-down, filtering, sorting or search (won't scale past ~12 rows); recommendations are generic ("Worth a closer look") rather than costed; terminology overload around "position"; no real PDF/board-pack export; no empty/error states.

3.5  Benefits report public/benefits/index.html

A 5,300-line standalone static HTML report inherited from "pengechurchesv3," with its own fonts, its own navy sidebar, and colour-coded category cards. Handsome as a document.

Showstopper

It still says "Penge Churches" throughout — a previous client's name hardcoded into the demo. This erodes trust instantly if seen, and is the first thing to fix (→ rebrand to Brighton Technologies).

It's also a different design system (typeface, nav, spacing) from the React app, and is not driven by the same config/data — editing it means hand-editing raw HTML, outside the CMS.

3.6  Design system & brand index.css · theme.ts · components/ui/*

A real, documented system: HSL tokens, a 5-level navy-tinted shadow scale, 12px base radius, tasteful animations, 57 shadcn/ui components. Genuinely premium foundations.

Loose ends: three fonts loaded but a typographic split across the product (Inter used, Fraunces loaded-but-unused, Plus Jakarta Sans only in shell chrome); a dark-mode variant defined but never activated; CSS duplication; chart colours drifting off-palette; unused brand PNGs in attached_assets/.

Redesign note

The new direction re-tokenises this system to navy (punctuation) + cream/white (field) + emerald accent, and unifies the Benefits report onto it.

4. Recommendations

Tagging: Quick win hours   Project days   Strategic product-defining bets

4.1  Coherence — make it feel like one premium product

  • Strategic Fold Pay & Benefits into the single React app (or at minimum make the transition seamless). Eliminate window.location full reloads; use client-side routing and a shared shell.
  • Quick win Purge the "Penge Churches" leftover and rebrand the whole demo as Brighton Technologies, driven from clientConfig, matching the Brighton-based roles in the Pay data.
  • Project Unify the design system across Pay, Benefits and Home — one typeface, one nav model, one token set, all on the existing Zigbert system (navy / gold / cream).
  • Quick win One navigation model applied everywhere; add breadcrumbs / "you are here".

4.2  Meaning over data — the highest-value theme

  • Strategic An "Your quarter in 3 things" insights engine. Replace static Highlights with data-derived headlines — the personalised headline of the whole engagement.
  • Strategic Prescriptive recommendations with cost. "X is 8% below median (£Y); moving N people to median costs ~£Z/yr." Turn Risks into an action list with a price tag — what justifies the engagement and seeds the upsell.
  • Project A genuine "Total Reward" view combining pay position and benefits value — what a consultancy can say that a salary survey can't.
  • Quick win Sharper, client-specific copy on every page.

4.3  Interactivity — earn the right to be a portal, not a PDF

  • Project Filter / sort / search on role and benefit tables.
  • Project Drill-down from any chart bar/role to the detail.
  • Strategic Scenario modelling ("what-if") — model "bring all below-median roles to median" and instantly see total cost and the new distribution. A genuine board-meeting "wow."
  • Strategic Comparison over time — quartile-by-quarter trend; "what changed since last report" so the portal compounds in value.
  • Project Real export — a branded, board-ready PDF/board-pack, plus "email me this report."

4.4  Trust & credibility

  • Quick win Data provenance & freshness everywhere — sources, sample sizes ("n="), "as at" dates, and a clear "sample data" badge on the demo.
  • Quick win Built-in methodology & glossary reachable from any term, so clients can defend the numbers to their board.
  • Quick win Confidence signalling — flag thin-sample roles.

4.5  Sign-in & account

  • Quick win Finish the auth screen — forgot-password, show/hide, validation, specific errors, remove the demo placeholder, and keep temp-auth creds out of real builds.
  • Project A real Account area — package/subscription, billing contact, data export, and a clean upgrade path where the Benefits card currently dead-ends.
  • Strategic White-glove first-run — a short guided tour and "how to read a quartile."

4.6  Aesthetics & finish

  • Quick win Bring charts onto brand — navy/gold/slate from the existing token set, not indigo.
  • Quick win Elevate the premium feel within the gold/cream/navy identity — richer depth and layered shadows, tighter spacing rhythm, type discipline, more considered hover/active/focus states, gold used with restraint as a true accent.
  • Quick win Tidy the design system — one font strategy, remove CSS duplication, ship or remove dark mode.
  • Project Use the brand imagery (or commission light editorial illustration) for identity beyond micro-interactions.
  • Project Mobile polish — carousel and rotated chart labels get cramped.
  • Quick win Accessibility pass — semantic structure, contrast, keyboard nav, ARIA on charts.

4.7  Admin / editor experience (the consultant's product)

  • Project Bring the Benefits report under the same CMS — no more hand-editing 5,000 lines of HTML.
  • Project A proper instance-setup flow (client name, package, branding, data load) — how TwentySix scales to many clients.
  • Quick win Richer edit affordances beyond plain-text inline editing.

4.8  Ambitious "make them love it" bets (the north star)

  • Decision-first dashboard — lead with "Your 3 priorities this quarter," each with impact + cost + one-click "add to action plan."
  • Action plan / tracker the client builds and revisits — a one-off report becomes an ongoing relationship surface.
  • Scenario & budget planner as the headline interactive feature.
  • Quarterly "what changed" digest + optional email when new data lands — a reason to return.
  • Board-pack generator — one click to a beautifully branded PDF the client presents as their own (TwentySix credited). The most shareable, upsell-generating feature.
  • Total-reward storytelling — pay + benefits woven into one narrative.

5. Suggested roadmap

Phase 0 — Integrity, rebrand & finish (quick wins, do first)

Rebrand everything to Brighton Technologies and purge "Penge Churches"; keep the gold/cream/navy identity and elevate its premium feel; bring the charts onto the existing brand (navy/gold); finish auth; add "sample data" + freshness/source badges; tidy fonts/CSS/dark-mode; add a glossary/methodology link.

Phase 1 — Coherence

Remove full-reload app switches; one nav model + breadcrumbs; re-skin/rebuild Benefits in the unified system; bring Benefits under config/CMS.

Phase 2 — Meaning

Data-derived Highlights/Hot Bar; prescriptive costed recommendations; sharper per-client copy; real account/upgrade path.

Phase 3 — Interactivity & the "wow"

Filter/sort/search + drill-down; branded PDF/board-pack export; comparison-over-time; scenario/budget modelling; total-reward view; first-run guided tour.

Verification

Run locally (npm run dev after rm node_modules && npm install, since node_modules is currently a symlink to Dashboard-temp), walk the full journey on desktop and mobile widths, confirm no cross-client data leaks, validate exports, and check brand/token consistency across the previously-separate Benefits app.
