Where Brighton Technologies stands today

Brighton Technologies's benefits offer sits comfortably in the housing-association market with four genuine upper-quartile strengths and one clear development area. The four strengths together build a strong recruitment narrative; the development area is where bespoke investment would have the most leverage.

Headline strengths
  • Pension — 10% employer DC sits at the HA upper quartile.
  • CIH membership for every employee + up to £500/yr professional fees — upper quartile.
  • HSF Health Plan + Perkbox bundle — combines a cash plan with retail rewards.
  • Compassionate & bereavement leave — tiered paid structure beyond statutory.
Areas to watch
  • Sick pay — entry-year SSP-only window sits below market; provision is generous post 2 years.
  • Personal development — general provision but no structured per-employee budget (sector median £200–£350/yr).
  • Salary sacrifice — live for pension only and not currently used; could be widened (EV, tech, cycle).

Building Strong Foundations

This page covers the foundational reward package across the housing-association sector: bonus, annual leave, additional days, buy & sell leave, employer pension, sick pay, maternity and paternity pay, car allowance and out-of-hours call-out. Each row shows the lower quartile, median and upper quartile of typical market practice — click any row to open the full per-quartile narrative and our market commentary.

Bonus 0% (no bonus scheme) 2–4% of salary 5–8% of salary
Lower Quartile
0% (no bonus scheme)
  • Lower quartile housing associations typically offer no annual bonus whatsoever.
  • These organisations — often smaller, community-based or operating in highly cost-constrained environments — rely on incremental pay progression, cost-of-living awards and sector-wide pay frameworks (such as those aligned to NJC or local agreements) rather than performance-related pay.
  • Where a bonus does exist at the lower quartile, it may be a flat-rate, non-consolidated 'thank you' payment of £250–£500 rather than a percentage of salary, often discretionary and non-contractual.
Median ★
2–4% of salary
  • Median-level housing associations operate a modest annual bonus scheme, typically in the range of 2–4% of salary, usually reserved for senior and middle management grades rather than all-staff.
  • Schemes at this level are generally discretionary, linked to a combination of organisational performance (e.g. customer satisfaction scores, void rates, development targets) and individual performance ratings.
  • Eligibility is commonly restricted by grade (e.g. Head of Service and above) and service length (usually 12 months' service by year-end), and payments are made as a non-consolidated cash sum.
Upper Quartile
5–8% of salary
  • Upper quartile housing associations — typically larger, more commercially oriented or group-structure organisations (e.g. those with significant development arms, market-sale or shared ownership activity) — operate more structured annual incentive plans with awards of 5–8% of salary for management, and occasionally 10–15% for director and executive levels.
  • Best-in-class employers extend eligibility to a broader employee population, adopt transparent scorecard frameworks, and link bonus targets to a balanced mix of social value KPIs (e.g. resident satisfaction, energy performance) alongside financial metrics.
  • A small number of leading employers now offer all-employee bonus or profit-sharing arrangements funded from surpluses.
Annual Leave 25 days 27–28 days 30–33 days
Lower Quartile
25 days
  • Lower quartile housing associations typically offer the statutory minimum-adjacent 25 days annual leave plus bank holidays (8 days), totalling 33 days.
  • Eligibility is usually from day one of employment with no service-related increments, and there is typically no option to buy or sell leave.
  • These are often smaller registered providers or those under significant financial pressure from the ongoing development and stock maintenance cost squeeze.
Median ★
27–28 days
  • Median employers in the social housing sector offer 27–28 days annual leave plus 8 bank holidays, totalling approximately 35–36 days.
  • Most include a modest length-of-service increment, typically adding 1 day per year of service up to a ceiling (e.g. 30 days after 3–5 years).
  • Flexible leave purchasing (buy up to 5 days) is increasingly common at this level, and most policies allow carry-over of up to 5 days into the next leave year.
Upper Quartile
30–33 days
  • Upper quartile and best-in-class housing associations offer 30–33 days as a starting entitlement, often rising to 35 days with service, plus 8 bank holidays.
  • Larger providers such as Clarion, L&Q, Peabody and Places for People regularly feature 30+ days as a headline offer.
  • These organisations frequently layer additional flexibility including buy/sell schemes (typically ±5 days), 'wellbeing days' (1–2 additional discretionary days), birthday leave, and enhanced carry-over or 'time in lieu' arrangements for occasional weekend working.
Additional Days 0 days (no formal wellbeing days policy) 1 day per year 2–3 days per year
Lower Quartile
0 days (no formal wellbeing days policy)
  • Lower quartile housing associations and social housing providers do not offer a formal standalone wellbeing day entitlement.
  • Wellbeing support at this level is typically limited to signposting to an Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) or occasional ad hoc manager-approved time off.
  • Where mental health or wellbeing leave exists, it is absorbed within sick leave or discretionary special leave provisions rather than as a named, contractual entitlement.
Median ★
1 day per year
  • The typical housing association or social housing employer offers 1 dedicated wellbeing day per year, usually taken as a full day and either contractual or firmly established as a discretionary entitlement.
  • This is commonly framed as a 'mental health day' or 'personal wellbeing day' and is separate from annual leave, although some employers allow it to be added to annual leave if unused.
  • Eligibility is generally available to all employees from day one or after a short qualifying period of around 3 months.
Upper Quartile
2–3 days per year
  • Upper quartile and leading social housing employers offer 2–3 wellbeing days per year as a contractual entitlement available to all staff from day one of employment.
  • These organisations often embed wellbeing days within a broader wellbeing framework that includes mental health first aiders, access to digital wellbeing platforms (e.g. Unmind, Headspace for Work), and line manager wellbeing training.
  • Some best-in-class employers in the sector allow wellbeing days to be taken flexibly in half-day increments and actively promote usage to reduce stigma around mental health-related absence.

Pension 5–6% employer contribution 8–10% employer contribution 12–15%+ employer contribution
Lower Quartile
5–6% employer contribution
  • Lower quartile housing associations typically offer employer pension contributions of 5–6% of salary, often through a defined contribution (DC) scheme following closure of legacy defined benefit (DB) arrangements.
  • These organisations commonly operate the minimum to remain competitive within sector, with eligibility usually from 3 months' service and no enhanced matching tiers.
  • Many smaller or financially constrained associations fall in this band, particularly those that have wound down SHPS (Social Housing Pension Scheme) DB participation and replaced it with a straightforward DC offering.
Median ★
8–10% employer contribution
  • The typical housing association employer contributes 8–10% of salary into a defined contribution pension, often through the SHPS DC section, NEST, or a master trust arrangement such as Aviva, Legal & General or Standard Life.
  • Contributions at this level usually apply from day one or after a short probationary period, and some associations operate a tiered matching structure (e.g. employer matches employee contributions up to a cap).
  • Legacy DB liabilities under SHPS remain a material cost for median employers, even where active DB accrual has ceased.
Upper Quartile
12–15%+ employer contribution
  • Upper quartile and best-in-class housing associations offer employer contributions of 12–15% or above, often retaining active defined benefit accrual through the SHPS DB section (career average revalued earnings/CARE basis) or offering a generous DC alternative.
  • Some larger, financially stable registered providers (e.g. larger G15-type associations or those with strong balance sheets) continue DB membership as a key retention tool for long-serving staff.
  • Top employers may also offer enhanced matching tiers, death-in-service top-ups linked to pension, and financial wellbeing education around retirement planning as part of an integrated approach.
Sick Pay 4 weeks (20 days) full pay, then 4 weeks half pay in any rolling 12-month period 8 weeks full pay, then 8 weeks half pay in any rolling 12-month period 6 months (26 weeks) full pay, then 6 months half pay, with service-linked enhancement
Lower Quartile
4 weeks (20 days) full pay, then 4 weeks half pay in any rolling 12-month period
  • Lower quartile housing associations typically offer 4 weeks full pay followed by 4 weeks half pay within a rolling 12-month period, reverting to Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) thereafter.
  • Eligibility usually requires completion of a probationary period (typically 3–6 months), and entitlement is non-contractual or set at the minimum enhanced level common to the sector.
  • Little or no service-linked enhancement is provided, and absence management policies tend to be applied more rigidly with Bradford Factor triggers used to control discretionary payment.
Median ★
8 weeks full pay, then 8 weeks half pay in any rolling 12-month period
  • The typical housing association offers 8 weeks full pay followed by 8 weeks half pay in any rolling 12-month period, with service-linked enhancement that increases entitlement after 2–3 years of service, commonly reaching 13 weeks full pay and 13 weeks half pay by year 5.
  • This is normally a contractual entitlement, stated in the contract of employment or employee handbook.
  • Occupational sick pay (OSP) applies from day one of sickness in most mid-market organisations, with SSP absorbed into the OSP payment, and there is typically no waiting day deduction.
Upper Quartile
6 months (26 weeks) full pay, then 6 months half pay, with service-linked enhancement
  • Upper quartile and best-in-class housing associations offer up to 6 months (26 weeks) full pay followed by 6 months half pay, often with full entitlement available from day one of employment (or after a short qualifying period of one month).
  • Service-linked enhancements may extend total entitlement beyond 12 months of combined full and half pay after 5+ years of service.
  • Leading employers supplement OSP with early-intervention wellbeing support, self-referral to occupational health from week two, and rehabilitation or phased return-to-work frameworks embedded in the sick pay policy, effectively reducing long-term absence costs while maintaining generous contractual provision.
Maternity Pay SMP only (statutory minimum: 6 weeks at 90% pay, then £184.03/week for 33 weeks) Full pay for 8–12 weeks, then SMP for remainder of qualifying period Full pay for 16–26 weeks, then half pay for a further 8–13 weeks, then SMP
Lower Quartile
SMP only (statutory minimum: 6 weeks at 90% pay, then £184.03/week for 33 weeks)
  • Lower quartile housing associations typically offer statutory maternity pay only — 6 weeks at 90% of average weekly earnings followed by 39 weeks at the SMP flat rate (£184.03/week from April 2024, rising to £187.18/week from April 2025).
  • These tend to be smaller or financially constrained registered providers with limited HR resource.
  • No occupational enhancement is provided beyond the legal minimum, and eligibility mirrors HMRC statutory criteria (26 weeks' service by the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth).
Median ★
Full pay for 8–12 weeks, then SMP for remainder of qualifying period
  • The typical housing association offers an occupational maternity scheme providing full pay (basic salary) for approximately 8 to 12 weeks, followed by SMP for the remaining statutory entitlement period.
  • Many schemes include a returner clause requiring the employee to return to work for a specified period (commonly 3 months) or repay the occupational enhancement.
  • Eligibility thresholds often require 12 months' continuous service, which is consistent with the broader public sector and not-for-profit norm.
Upper Quartile
Full pay for 16–26 weeks, then half pay for a further 8–13 weeks, then SMP
  • Upper quartile and best-in-class housing associations — typically larger G15 or L&Q-scale providers and those with strong EDI commitments — offer full pay for 16 to 26 weeks followed by half pay for a further 8 to 13 weeks, then SMP to complete the 39-week statutory period.
  • Some leading employers are moving toward 26 weeks' full pay with no service requirement or reduced service thresholds of 6 months, aligning with progressive private sector practice.
  • A small number of sector leaders have also introduced equal parental pay policies that bring paternity and shared parental pay in line with maternity, reflecting a whole-family approach.
Paternity Pay Statutory Paternity Pay (SPP) only — 2 weeks at £187.18/week (2025/26 rate) 2 weeks full pay, followed by SPP for remainder of statutory entitlement 4–6 weeks full pay, some extending to 8 weeks or offering shared parental leave matching maternity pay
Lower Quartile
Statutory Paternity Pay (SPP) only — 2 weeks at £187.18/week (2025/26 rate)

  • There is no contractual enhancement, meaning employees earning above the SPP rate receive a significant pay drop.
  • Eligibility conditions mirror statutory requirements — 26 weeks service by the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth — with no discretionary flexibility.

2 weeks full pay, followed by SPP for remainder of statutory entitlement
  • The median housing association enhances paternity/partner pay to 2 weeks at full contractual pay, aligning with the statutory leave period but removing the pay reduction for most employees.
  • This is typically contractual and available after a qualifying service period of 6–12 months, though some employers apply no service requirement beyond statutory.
  • It is generally extended to partners and same-sex couples but may not yet automatically include adoptive parents or those using surrogacy in all policies.
Upper Quartile
4–6 weeks full pay, some extending to 8 weeks or offering shared parental leave matching maternity pay
  • Upper quartile employers in the social housing sector offer 4–6 weeks at full pay, with some leading organisations extending to 8 weeks or more.
  • Best-in-class practice includes pay-matching arrangements where paternity/partner pay mirrors the first phase of enhanced maternity pay, explicit inclusion of adoption and surrogacy, and full alignment with Shared Parental Leave policies — allowing partners to receive the same enhanced rate when taking SPL.
  • A small number of progressive housing associations have introduced gender-neutral parental leave policies removing the distinction between primary and secondary carer pay entirely.

  • Best-in-class organisations also offer enhanced mileage rates above the HMRC Advisory Rate for the first tranche of business miles, or provide fuel cards and charging infrastructure access for EV drivers.

Large Private Sector — typical practice

PMI is the dominant health benefit in large private — around 84% offer it, typically with family-cover options and dental, optical and mental-health riders. PHI / Group Income Protection is standard alongside, plus digital GP, annual health screening and structured gym subsidies.

Less Common Benefits & Ideas Worth Noting

  • Free fruit or other food (this was very common at one point but appears to have fallen out of favour)
  • Programmes to encourage employee health, physical fitness and relaxation courses
  • Menopause & fertility support
