## Where we are now

> Two or three sentences, no more. The detail is in the appendix. Overall position and
> the count below market are both on the Pay dashboard's headline; the pay bill is the
> **Total salary bill** tile.

Across our {roles} benchmarked roles we pay at about the market median overall. That
headline hides the thing worth acting on: {below_median} of {roles} roles sit below the
median for their function, level and location, and the gaps are concentrated in
Engineering and Data.

Our overall figure sits at the median while nine roles sit below it because a handful of
well-paid senior roles offset them. This is normal and it is not an error in the data.
It is also the reason a single average is a poor basis for a pay decision.

> A board will ask about that apparent contradiction, usually in a way that stalls the
> meeting. Answering it before it is asked costs you two sentences and buys you the
> rest of the discussion.

## Why this needs a decision now

> Make the risk concrete and local. Replace these with the roles that are actually
> hard to fill or that you have lost people from. If you have recruitment or turnover
> evidence, this is where it belongs: the market data shows the gap, your own
> experience shows it is biting.

Roles below the median are where retention risk concentrates. We are carrying gaps in
six of our engineering and data roles, which are the roles we have found hardest to
recruit into over the past year.

Nothing here is a compliance problem. No role sits below the market lower quartile, so
this is a decision about how competitive we choose to be, not about correcting a floor.

## What we recommend

> State the position, then the reason. If your organisation has a written pay
> positioning, quote it here and the argument is already made. If it does not, this
> paper is the moment to set one.

We recommend the median. It is the smallest change that removes a defensible complaint
from everyone in those {below_median} roles, it costs {uplift_median_pct}% of the pay
bill, and it leaves us able to argue that we pay the going rate for every role we
employ.

We are not recommending the upper quartile. At {uplift_uq} it is four times the cost,
and paying above three quarters of the market is a position that needs a reason beyond
recruitment difficulty in two functions.

## What we are not asking for

> Short, and worth including. Naming the limits of the ask is what stops a board
> widening it, and it is the fastest way to look like you have thought about it.

This paper covers base salary only. It does not cover bonus, benefits, or any change to
our pay structure or grading. It does not create an expectation of an annual uplift of
this size.

## Where these numbers come from

> Leave this in. It is the section that keeps the meeting on the decision instead of
> on the data, and it is the answer to "how do we know that is the market rate".

The market ranges are from Zigbert, our pay benchmarking subscription, built from a
database of over {salary_records} UK salary records collected from live job adverts and
refreshed every month. Each of our roles is matched on function, job level, industry
and location rather than on job title, and every benchmark is reviewed by a reward
specialist at TwentySix Consulting before it reaches us.
