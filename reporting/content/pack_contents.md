## What is in this pack

Seven things, and you do not need all of them at once. The order below is the order we
suggest.

| What you have | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **A 60-second film** | What the dashboard is, in the time it takes to read this page. Start here. |
| **This pack** | What you have, what to look at first, what we need from you, and what happens next. |
| **Zigbert Benchmarking Template** *(spreadsheet)* | Where you list the roles you want benchmarked. The only thing we need back from you. |
| **How to read your dashboard** | What the numbers and the labels mean. Read it once, then keep it for the moment a figure needs explaining. |
| **Your data, and how we look after it** | What we hold, where, who sees it and for how long. For whoever asks, and somebody usually does. |
| **A pay review board paper** *(Word template)* | A skeleton for the paper you write when you need a pay decision approved, with a worked example alongside it. |
| **The Zigbert one-pager** | A single page explaining what Zigbert is. Useful for forwarding to colleagues who ask what you have bought. |

### In what order

**Today.** Watch the film, then read the next two pages: how the process works, and
what you have. That is the whole of what you need to get started.

**Before your dashboard is built.** Fill in the spreadsheet and email it back. Your
dashboard follows within {turnaround}.

**Your first week with the dashboard.** Check your own roles are right, take the tours,
and look at what sits below the market median.

**When a number needs explaining.** *How to read your dashboard.* It covers the two or
three things that trip everyone up the first time.

**When you need a decision approved.** The board paper template. It exists because the
dashboard can tell you what a pay review would cost but cannot make the case for it
inside your organisation.
