## What you have

Your Zigbert dashboard is a live view of where your pay and benefits sit against the market. It is not a report that goes out of date on the shelf: the market data behind it refreshes every month, and your own figures update the moment you change them.

There are six areas along the top.

| Area | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Where you stand overall, and what to act on first |
| Pay | Role by role against the market, and a pay review modeller |
| Benefits | Your {benefits} benefits across {categories} categories, benchmarked |
| Trends and Hotspots | What the wider market is doing, and where pay pressure is building |
| Methodology | Where the numbers come from and how they are produced |
| Your Organisation | Your roles, people and benefits, and where you keep them current |

Account and billing sit under your name in the top right.

### Signing in

Use the username and password we sent you. There is no self-registration, deliberately: your consultant manages access so that nobody can add themselves to your organisation's data. If a password needs resetting, or somebody joins or leaves your team, email us and we will do it.

Anything you see marked **Illustrative sample data** is exactly that, and is not your organisation's real figures. That badge disappears once your own benchmarks are live.

## The first five minutes

Three things, in this order, and you will know where you stand.

**One. The sentence under the greeting on Home.** It is generated from your own data every time the page loads, not written by anyone. It tells you how your pay sits against the market median, how many roles are worth reviewing, and how your benefits compare. If you read one thing, read that.

**Two. What needs attention, further down Home.** Up to six items, ranked from your data rather than chosen by us. Each row names the role, the gap and the function, and opens the detail behind it.

**Three. Your own roles, on Your Organisation.** Check the list is right before you act on anything else. Salaries move, people join, titles change, and a benchmark against a stale salary is worse than no benchmark. This is the one page where you are the authority and we are not.

### The guided tours

Each area has a short tour of its own, between twenty and forty-five seconds, offered the first time you arrive there. They are separate on purpose: an explanation lands better next to the thing it explains than five minutes before you reach it.

Home keeps a **Getting started** panel showing which areas you have toured. It disappears once you have seen all five, and you can dismiss it at any time. The **Tour this page** button in the top bar brings the tour back whenever you want it.

The tours cover where things are. The companion guide, *How to read your dashboard*, covers what the numbers mean. They do not overlap.

## Keeping your data current

Your Organisation is the only page where you change things, and everything you change there flows straight through to the rest of the dashboard.

**By role or by person.** Most organisations start by role: one salary, one market range, one position. Switch to **By person** when several people share a job title and their pay differs, or when anyone works part time. The toggle sits at the top of the page and in the Pay dashboard, and the two stay in step.

**Editing.** Change a salary or a detail and it updates immediately: the Home verdict, the Pay dashboards, the attention list. There is no separate publish step. If you want to explore a change rather than commit to it, use **Prep a Pay Review** in the Pay dashboard, which models the cost without touching your data.

**Adding.** Add a role, a person or a benefit and it goes to your consultant to benchmark. It shows as *Awaiting benchmark* until the market data comes back, which takes **{turnaround}**.

### What a quarterly refresh changes, and what it does not

The market data underneath your dashboard is refreshed every month. Your dashboard itself is rebuilt from it, reviewed by one of our reward specialists, and republished **once a quarter**, which is what your subscription covers. You are currently reading {report_period}, published {last_updated}.

A refresh changes the market ranges. It does not change anything you have entered: your roles, salaries, people and benefits are yours, and a refresh moves the market around them rather than overwriting them.

Benefits run a cycle behind pay, because they come from survey and audit evidence rather than job adverts. The benefits figures here are from {benefits_updated}.

If the market moves sharply mid-quarter and you need a rebuild sooner, ask. We can arrange one.
