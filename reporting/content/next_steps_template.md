## What we need from you

To benchmark a role we need five things about it. Nothing more, and nothing commercially sensitive beyond the salary itself.

| Field | Why we need it |
| --- | --- |
| Role title | The starting point, though never the whole story: two roles with the same title in different organisations are frequently not the same job |
| Current FTE salary | The full-time equivalent annual salary. This is what gets compared to the market range |
| Experience level | Which seniority bracket the role sits in |
| Function or job family | The department the role belongs to, such as Engineering, Finance or Commercial |
| Location | Regional pay varies materially, and London and the South East are treated separately |

The four experience levels are **Entry or Foundation**, **Early and Developing**, **Mid to Senior**, and **Experts, Strategists and Leaders**. If a role sits awkwardly between two, say so and we will look at it rather than guess.

We also need four things about the organisation once, at the start: its name, its industry, its total number of employees, and how many roles you want assessed.

### Two ways to send it

**The spreadsheet, for anything more than a handful.** We will send you *Zigbert Benchmarking Template.xlsx*. It has an Organisation tab and a Roles tab, takes up to 500 roles, and the Experience Level column is a dropdown so the values always match ours. Fill in the highlighted cells and email it back.

**The dashboard, for ones and twos.** Once you are set up, **Add a role** on Your Organisation does the same job for a single role, and is the quicker route when you are hiring into something new.

Benefits benchmarking is included in every package. Gender pay gap analysis follows as an
add-on by the end of 2026.

Headcount per role is optional but worth giving us. It lets us show your total pay bill and model what a pay review would actually cost, rather than only where each role sits.

## What happens next

Steps 4 and 5 on the process page are the ones worth understanding, because they are the part that makes the numbers defensible.

Each role is matched against our database of more than {salary_records} UK salary records, then narrowed to a comparator group that genuinely resembles you: **{pay_employers} technology employers** advertising comparable roles in {pay_basis}. A narrow comparator group is the point. A wider one would be a bigger number and a worse benchmark.

Then a reward specialist reviews the result and confirms it makes sense. That review is why this is not a search result. Self-service pay tools can return figures that simply do not look right, and without reward expertise it is hard to know which to trust. Nobody sees a Zigbert dashboard that a specialist has not signed off.

### Then, over the first month

**Check your own data first.** Go to Your Organisation and confirm the roster is right. Everything else in the dashboard is computed from it.

**Take the tours.** Five short ones, roughly two and a half minutes in total.

**Look at the roles below the lower quartile.** These are where retention risk concentrates and where acting early is cheapest.

**Model a review before you plan a budget.** Prep a Pay Review shows what it would cost to move roles to the lower quartile, the median or the upper quartile. It is much easier to argue for a number when you can show what it buys.

**Read the market context.** Trends and Hotspots covers what the wider market is doing, which is what turns a position into a decision.

## When the dashboard is enough, and when it is not

We would rather tell you where this fits than oversell it.

**The dashboard handles well:** a market check on established roles, a standard annual pay review, validating a salary for a new hire, understanding your overall position, and watching the market move underneath you.

**Ask us about bespoke benchmarking when:** a role is genuinely complex, hybrid or unusual; you need a full pay structure review rather than role-by-role rates; recruitment is failing in a way the market rate does not explain; you want costed recommendations and help implementing them; or you are going through a restructure.

Bespoke work is a different service with a different depth of evidence, and it is the right answer often enough that we would rather point you to it than stretch the dashboard to cover it.
